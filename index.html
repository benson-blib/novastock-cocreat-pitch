What the structure of this market tells us

  • Goods move importers → wholesalers → retailers. Lubumbashi is one of the country's principal distribution centres.
  • The informal share of that distribution grows whenever the economy tightens, and many trading firms already run part of their business through informal channels.
  • Small Congolese businesses are, in the ITA's own words, "generally unable to obtain credit" and "often ignored by chambers of commerce".
  • The government's own National Financial Inclusion Strategy 2023–2028 targets moving financial inclusion from 38.5% (2022) to 65% by 2028.

A dollar locked in overstock is a dollar of working capital gone, in a market where credit is out of reach. These shops already keep the record — what they cannot finish is the decision.

Then

The reorder rule fits on one line.

Reorder when the shelf holds no more than the units you will sell before the supplier delivers, plus a safety margin. The owner can check every number, and so can you.

  • Inputs the owner already knows: what sells per day, and how long the supplier takes.
  • Reasons you can audit: every figure in the answer traces back to a number on the shelf or a delivery the owner has already waited for.
  • The prototype at the top of this page uses exactly this rule, on sample data.

What we are not claiming: this is decades-old inventory theory, not an invention. What is new is making it operable at the informal last mile — offline, with no barcodes — and making it explainable enough that an owner acts on it.

Worked example: cooking oil (sample data)

Sales3 units a day
Supplier lead time4 days, plus 2 safety days
Reorder point3 × (4 + 2) = 18 units
On the shelf9 units — below the point, so reorder
Order quantity3 × (4 + 2 + 7) − 9 = 30 units

The 7 is a week of extra selling, so the owner is not back at the supplier the next day.

We are not building another B2B distributor.

The model that buys, stores and delivers the stock has already been tried across Africa at scale — with a lot of capital, and a hard landing.

What happened next door

  • Wasoko (Kenya) and MaxAB (Egypt) merged into what was billed as Africa's largest B2B commerce platform, after a reported $501m valuation for Wasoko.
  • Alerzo (Nigeria) restructured and cut staff.
  • eBee (Kenya) reportedly laid off most of its ~50 employees in early 2025.
  • Twiga Foods (Kenya), which had raised $160m, is regularly cited among the sector's distressed names.

What we do instead

  • No stock. No trucks. No inventory risk. No working capital tied up in goods we own.
  • We sell the decision that precedes the order, and we earn only when an order actually happens.
  • The decision is the product, not the record: a stock file without lead times cannot produce a signal, and without the signal the order stays a guess.

None of those companies is focused on DR Congo, and none of them is our competitor in Lubumbashi. The alternatives our first customers actually use are an Excel file on a counter PC, a wholesaler they know, and a notebook — and the spreadsheet is exactly where we start.

Payment that works the way Africa actually pays.

No wire transfers, no foreign bank accounts. Checkout is designed around mobile money and card, because that is already how these owners transact every day.

  • No bank account required: pay international suppliers from a mobile wallet.
  • Cross-border made local: NovaStock converts and settles with the supplier, and the owner sees one simple payment.
  • Built for low connectivity: USSD and lightweight mobile data, not only fast broadband.
  • Crossing the border is a real step, not an afterthought: importing into DR Congo means customs and clearance, and Kasumbalesa on the Zambian border is one of the country's main entry points. Working out who handles that paperwork is part of this step, not a detail we have skipped.

Payments are roadmap, not product. Moving money across borders is a licensed activity, and the DRC introduced a new authorisation regime for digital platforms and fintechs in March 2026. We will not hold customer funds: the design routes settlement through a licensed provider, and we will take legal advice before this step ships.
